Tap to Pay: take card payments on your phone

Tap to Pay lets a compatible smartphone accept contactless cards and digital wallets directly, with no extra hardware. It’s ideal for occasional sales, back-up, queue-busting and getting started.

Photo: Nathan Dumlao on Unsplash

How Tap to Pay works

The customer taps their contactless card, phone or watch on the back of your phone. A payment app from your provider handles the payment, so you need an account with a provider that supports Tap to Pay. On iPhone it requires an iPhone XS or later; on Android it needs a phone with NFC that meets the provider’s requirements.

In the UK, Revolut Business and NatWest’s Tyl were launch partners for Tap to Pay on iPhone, and other providers including SumUp, Square and PayPal Point of Sale have added Tap to Pay to their apps. Check each provider’s app for current support on your device.

Contactless limits on Tap to Pay

Card contactless limits apply in the same way as on a terminal. In March 2026 the FCA removed the fixed £100 limit, letting card issuers set their own, so limits now vary. Payments from phones and watches with Face ID, fingerprint or passcode checks weren’t subject to the £100 cap. Read the contactless limit explained.

Frequently asked questions

Is Tap to Pay safe?
Yes. Payments are encrypted and card numbers aren’t stored on your phone. The provider handles security, as with a card reader.
Does Tap to Pay cost more than a card reader?
Usually not: most providers charge their normal in-person rate. Check your provider’s pricing page.
