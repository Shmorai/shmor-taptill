Fees explained

Card machine fees explained

Every card payment has a cost. Here’s where the money goes, what each fee on a quote means, and how to work out which pricing model is cheapest for your takings.

Where the money goes on every card payment

When a customer pays by card, three parties take a share of the fee you pay:

  • Interchange: paid to the customer’s card issuer. For UK consumer cards it is capped at 0.2% for debit and 0.3% for credit under the UK’s Interchange Fee Regulation. Business and many international cards are not capped the same way and cost more.
  • Scheme fees: paid to Visa, Mastercard and other card schemes.
  • Acquirer margin: what your card machine provider or acquirer keeps for processing, the terminal, support and risk.

Pay-as-you-go providers wrap all three into one flat percentage. Contract providers may show them separately (often called interchange++ or interchange-plus-plus) or as blended rates per card type.

On flat rates the maths is simple: £5,000 of monthly card takings costs £84.50 at 1.69% or £87.50 at 1.75%.

Flat rate or quoted rate: which is cheaper?

Flat rates are easy to budget but include a margin on every card. A quoted rate can be lower per transaction, but monthly fees and per-transaction charges come on top. To compare fairly:

  1. Take a recent month’s takings and number of transactions (your current statement shows both).
  2. Work out the flat-rate cost: takings × rate.
  3. Work out the quoted cost: (takings × quoted rate) + (transactions × authorisation fee) + monthly fees.
  4. Repeat for a quiet month, to see the effect of minimum charges.

Subscription plans sit in between. SumUp’s Payments Plus costs £19 a month for a headline 0.99% rate, which beats 1.69% pay as you go once eligible card takings pass about £2,714 a month (£19 ÷ 0.70%). See our worked example.

Five ways to cut your card fees

  • Get quotes once a year, using your latest statement. Large acquirers must remind you when your minimum term ends.
  • Remove authorisation fees if your average sale is small, or choose a flat rate.
  • Don’t pay for idle terminals: return machines you don’t use.
  • Complete your PCI questionnaire to avoid non-compliance fees.
  • Don’t surcharge consumers to recover fees: it’s not allowed. See the rules.

Frequently asked questions

What are typical card machine fees in the UK?
Pay-as-you-go providers publish flat rates of around 1.69% to 1.75% for in-person UK cards (October 2026). Contract rates are quoted individually and can be lower for higher volumes, but usually come with a monthly terminal fee and sometimes authorisation fees.
What is interchange++ pricing?
Interchange-plus-plus shows the three parts of the fee separately: the issuer’s interchange, the card scheme fee, and the acquirer’s margin. It’s more transparent, but statements are harder to read and your cost varies with the cards customers use.
Are card fees tax deductible?
Card processing fees are generally an allowable business expense. Check with your accountant or HMRC guidance for your situation.
Can I pass card fees on to customers?
Not for consumer debit and credit cards: surcharges have been banned since January 2018. Different rules apply to commercial cards. Read can you charge for card payments?
