Five questions that narrow the choice

  1. Where do you take payments? A fixed counter suggests a terminal or EPOS; on the move suggests a 4G mobile reader or Tap to Pay on a phone.
  2. How steady is your trade? Seasonal or occasional sellers often suit pay as you go with no monthly fee; busy, steady sites may find a quoted rate or subscription cheaper. Our pay-as-you-go vs subscription post explains the trade-off.
  3. Do you need more than payments? Stock, tables, bookings, staff and tips are EPOS territory; otherwise a simple terminal is enough.
  4. Where should the money land? Some providers pay into any UK business account, others only into their own, which can change costs.
  5. What is the contract? Check the minimum term, hardware ownership and cancellation fees before signing. See switching provider.

Checks that apply to every business type

  • Ask for the quote summary box. It sets out your key costs in a standard format; see how to read it.
  • No card surcharges on consumers. Businesses cannot add a surcharge for paying by card with consumers: what you can charge.
  • Plan for disputes. Every business taking cards will eventually meet a chargeback: what to do.
  • Compare like for like. Providers are listed alphabetically and unranked on this site, so use the same turnover and average sale when comparing quotes.

Frequently asked questions

What is the cheapest way for a small business to take card payments?
It depends on your turnover and average sale. Occasional or low-volume sellers often find pay as you go with no monthly fee cheaper, while busier businesses may do better with a quoted rate. Compare using the same figures for each provider.
Do I need an EPOS system or just a card machine?
A card machine is enough if you only need to take payments. An EPOS system adds stock, reporting, tables, staff and integrations, and is worth considering as your operation gets more complex.
Can a market trader take card payments without a landline?
Yes. Mobile readers connect over 4G or Wi-Fi via an app, and some phones can accept contactless payments directly.
