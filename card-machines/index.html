Pay as you go or contract?

UK card machine providers fall into two camps:

  • Pay as you go (flat rate): you buy a reader once and pay a fixed percentage per sale, with no monthly fee and no contract. SumUp, Square, PayPal Point of Sale and Lloyds Cardnet’s mPOS reader work this way. Simple and predictable, and usually cheapest for lower volumes.
  • Quoted / contract: you rent a terminal for a monthly fee and get rates set for your business, often varying by card type, sometimes with authorisation fees. Dojo, Teya, takepayments, Worldpay, Elavon and the banks’ terminal plans work this way. Busier businesses with larger average sales can often pay less overall, but you must read the whole price.

As a rough guide, once your monthly card takings reach a few thousand pounds, it’s worth getting quotes. Our fees guide shows how to compare a flat rate with a quote, and this worked example shows when a subscription plan pays off.

Questions to ask before you sign

  1. What is my rate for each card type, and is there an authorisation fee per transaction?
  2. What is the monthly terminal fee, and is the terminal rented, leased or bought?
  3. How long is the minimum term, and what does it cost to leave early?
  4. Are there PCI compliance fees, minimum monthly service charges or account fees?
  5. When do payouts arrive, and do I need to use your business account?
  6. Does the terminal integrate with my EPOS system?

Large acquirers must give small businesses a standard summary box of prices. Ask for it, and compare like with like: see how to read a card machine quote.

Who owns whom

Several well-known brands share an owner, which can matter when you switch:

  • Global Payments owns Worldpay (since January 2026) and takepayments.
  • PayPal owns Zettle, now being renamed PayPal Point of Sale.
  • Block owns Square.
  • Fiserv owns Clover, an EPOS and payments platform sold through partners.
  • Dojo is the trading brand of the company previously known as Paymentsense.

Frequently asked questions

Which card machine is best for a small business?
For low or variable takings, a pay-as-you-go reader with no monthly fee is usually the simplest and cheapest place to start. As takings grow, compare quotes from contract providers and look at subscription plans like SumUp Payments Plus. See card machine fees.
Is SumUp cheaper than Square or Zettle?
On published pay-as-you-go rates for in-person UK cards, SumUp’s 1.69% is slightly lower than the 1.75% charged by Square and PayPal Point of Sale (October 2026). Hardware prices, software, payout speed and extra fees also matter, so compare the whole package.
Why don’t some providers publish their prices?
Contract providers such as Dojo, Teya and the big acquirers usually price each business individually, based on turnover, sector, average sale and card mix. Ask for a written quote and the standard summary box.
