Global Payments now owns Worldpay: what it means for UK merchants

One of the biggest payment deals in years completed in January 2026, putting Worldpay under the same owner as takepayments. If you use either, here’s what to know.

By the TapTill editorial team · 15 October 2026 · Updated 16 October 2026 · 1 min read

Photo: rupixen on Unsplash

What happened

On 12 January 2026, US payments firm Global Payments completed its purchase of Worldpay from FIS and private equity firm GTCR, and at the same time sold its card-issuing business to FIS. The deal was first announced in April 2025. Global Payments says the combined business serves more than six million merchant locations worldwide.

In the UK, Global Payments already owned card machine provider takepayments, so two well-known small-business brands now sit in the same group.

What it means if you’re a Worldpay or takepayments customer

  • Your contract stays in force. A change of owner doesn’t change the terms you signed. Prices and terms can only change in line with your agreement, usually with notice.
  • Watch your inbox: integrations of this size can bring new statements, portals, terminals or terms over time. Read any change notices carefully, as they may give you a right to end your contract without a fee.
  • Check your renewal date: large acquirers must remind small businesses before their minimum term ends. That’s your moment to compare quotes.

Does it affect switching?

If you get quotes from Worldpay and takepayments, remember they now have the same parent company. For a truly independent comparison, include providers from other groups as well, such as Dojo, Teya, Barclaycard, Lloyds Cardnet, Elavon, SumUp, Square or PayPal Point of Sale.
