Can you charge customers for paying by card?

Card fees eat into margins, so it’s tempting to pass them on. In the UK, the law says no for most consumer payments. Here’s what you can and can’t do.

By the TapTill editorial team · 14 October 2026 · Updated 16 October 2026 · 1 min read

Photo: Mohrez Labaf on Unsplash

On this page

  1. The short answer
  2. What’s banned
  3. What’s still allowed
  4. Better ways to cut card costs

The short answer

Since 13 January 2018, UK businesses have been banned from charging consumers extra for paying with consumer debit or credit cards, and for most other common electronic payment methods. The rules are in the Consumer Rights (Payment Surcharges) Regulations 2012, as amended.

What’s banned

  • Adding a fee for paying by a consumer debit or credit card, in a shop, online or over the phone.
  • Surcharges on many other consumer payment methods covered by the rules, such as many e-money and payment-account transfers.
  • Disguising a surcharge as a “service fee” that only applies to card payers.

What’s still allowed

  • Commercial (business) cards: you may be able to surcharge payments made with a commercial card, but only up to the direct cost to you, and you must show it clearly. GOV.UK’s guidance explains the details.
  • Discounts for other methods: offering a lower price for cash or bank transfer is generally treated differently from a surcharge. Make sure prices are clear and not misleading.
  • Optional service charges and tips: a clearly shown, optional service charge that applies regardless of payment method isn’t a card surcharge. If staff receive tips, the Employment (Allocation of Tips) Act 2023 applies: see hospitality guide.
  • Minimum card spends: UK law doesn’t ban them, but card scheme rules and your provider’s terms may restrict them. Check before you put up a sign.

Better ways to cut card costs

Instead of surcharging, reduce the cost at source: compare rates once a year, remove per-transaction authorisation fees if your sales are small, and return terminals you don’t use. See card machine fees and switching provider.

Last updated: 16 October 2026

Frequently asked questions

Can I charge a fee for paying by credit card?
Not for consumer credit cards, since January 2018. Surcharges on commercial cards may be allowed up to your direct cost.
What happens if a business surcharges illegally?
Customers can ask for a refund of the surcharge, and Trading Standards can take action. Report concerns to Citizens Advice.
